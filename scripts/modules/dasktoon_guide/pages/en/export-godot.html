Export to Godot

The same Engine Export for Godot 4: the model as glTF, the materials as Godot shaders, the rig and the clips, ready to drag into a scene.

Running it

Choose File › Export › Engine Export…, set Engine to Godot 4 and pick a folder. In a Godot project (a folder with project.godot) it writes under res://DaskToon, and Godot imports it; otherwise it writes <Name>_Godot/DaskToon to copy into the root of the Godot project. The options are those of the Unity export; the model is a .glb file. Drag it into a scene: Godot puts the materials on it, and the rig gives it swaying hair and skirts, eyes, mouth and bend fixes.

Light and shadows

Set the DirectionalLight3D as the report says (energy is the Sun's strength divided by π). Set its Shadow Normal Bias to 4, and turn on MSAA 3D in the project's settings: with Godot's defaults the eyelids and fringe leave speckled shadows on the face and thin outlines break into dots.

The finish layer

When Illustration Finish is on, the export also writes <Name>_finish.tscn: add it to your scene to get the Shadow and Light Tint, Light Gradient, Saturation and Contrast and Vignette layers over the picture. The layers that blur or read the depth do not go to Godot; the report names those that are on.

What is different

Godot does not give the shaders ambient occlusion, so the AO switch draws nothing there. Where several lamps light one spot, Godot takes the brightest lamp's shading instead of adding them.
