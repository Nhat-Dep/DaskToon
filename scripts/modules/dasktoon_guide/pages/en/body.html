2. Body

Model the body standing up, the way the auto rig expects to find it. On the floor of the 3D view the red line is the X axis and the green one the Y axis; the front of the scene is -Y, the side the Front view (Numpad 1) looks at.

Size

Make the character 0.5 to 3.5 m tall; a person is about 1.6 to 1.8 m. DaskToon and Unity count in metres: a character 100 times too big gets springs, lights and physics 100 times too weak. The height is in the sidebar's Item tab, Dimensions Z. Fix scales the character to 1.6 m about its feet.

On the floor

The soles touch z = 0. Animations put the feet on the game's floor, so a character modelled above it floats. Fix moves it onto the floor.

In the middle

Stand where the red and green lines cross (x = 0, y = 0). The rig mirrors left and right across x = 0, and Unity turns the character about that point. Fix moves it there.

Facing the front

The face and the toes point to -Y, toward the Front view. The rig names the sides by it: a character facing the back gets its left arm called right. DaskToon reads the way it faces from the feet, so give them toes that point forward. Fix turns it to the front.

T or A pose

Model the arms out to the sides: level (a T) or up to 45° below level (an A). The auto rig finds the arms by how far they reach; arms hanging along the body are taken for the body. There is no Fix: pose the arms out in Edit Mode before rigging.

Even scale

Scale objects the same on X, Y and Z, or apply the scale (Object › Apply › Scale). A stretched or mirrored (negative) scale bends the weights and the export. Fix applies it.

Faces point outward

Turn on Overlays › Face Orientation: blue faces point outward, red ones inward. Inward faces make the rig give vertices to the wrong bones, and flip the outline and the shading. Fix turns them outward, like Mesh › Normals › Recalculate Outside in Edit Mode.
